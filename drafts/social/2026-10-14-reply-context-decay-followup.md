You read the tree closer than I did, all three of those are exactly where you said they are, and the honest answer to my own question is no, the diff alone doesn't hold up…

The part that stung is that the confirmation event you're asking for already exists and we throw the timestamp away. The retro pass re-reads every card in the phase's scope and asks whether that phase's events confirmed or contradicted it, confirmed moves the grade up a step, contradicted drops it to low and writes a corrected card. That's your "last confirmed" signal, firing on a real cadence, and the card never learns it happened, the frontmatter has `created` and nothing else date-shaped. So a card graded high and re-confirmed last week is indistinguishable from one graded high the day it was written and never looked at since. The fix I'm filing is a `confirmed` date on the card, set at write, bumped by any retro re-grade including "same grade, still true," and a second bucket in the memory audit: cards with no provenance whose last confirmation is older than the window, any grade, surfaced for the retro to re-confirm or retire. Age still only selects candidates and nothing leaves on a timer, which was the promise, but the cards your comment describes stop being invisible to it.

Last use I'm leaving out on purpose, and not because it's wrong, our recall isn't a ranked lookup. Every card in scope lands in the session banner as an index row and bodies get fetched by id, so "used" would just mean "a session happened," no signal in it. Which also surfaced a bug while I was checking: the banner tells the agent to fetch one body by id and the recall tool doesn't take an id, it takes scopes, so that call quietly returns every card in scope and the token estimate printed beside it is off by the card count. Your comment found that one by accident, it's going in the tracker today…

Your gRPC example settles the supersedes question. Same shape as the retro gap, actually: the contradicted-card path already writes the corrected card as a new card, at exactly the moment the correction knows which card it's closing, and we don't record the link. Issues in cairn have a supersedes edge and a lineage walk, cards don't. So: `supersedes` on card create, recall returns `supersededBy` on the old card the way it returns `stale`, the banner lists superseded cards out of the live table, and the compaction archive carries the lineage. And "drop it to low" stops being the proxy for "closed," which is your table, those were never the same operation.

For what it's worth, our own store is 57 cards and every one carries provenance, but only because the retro mints them from the ledger range and the ledger always has files and commits. The write paths with no ledger behind them, a remembered convention, a note from an incident, are where the unbacked cards would come from, and that's the population your comment is about.
